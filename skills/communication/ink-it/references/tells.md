# Machine tells

Adapted from Ghostwriter's tells pass (see `../PROVENANCE.md`). It applies to Slack, email and PR comments, not to PR titles/bodies or commit messages. Run it on your own draft before review. It removes what marks prose as machine-written, not what makes it the user's. A word or habit the voice profile uses is never a tell.

Test: a model writes for the widest reader; a person writes for one. A sentence that could sit unchanged in someone else's message says nothing about this one.

Weight the tells. Structure tells earn an edit at one sighting. A lone hedge or passive is a habit until other tells share the passage.

## Punctuation

- Punctuation follows the profile. If it bans em dashes, use what the profile uses instead: a spaced hyphen ` - `, a double hyphen ` -- `, a semicolon, a comma or a new sentence. Local change from upstream: Ghostwriter bans em dashes, spaced hyphens and `--` for everyone; here bans come from the user's profile, and the runner enforces only those listed in the user's `rules.json`.
- Emoji follow the profile the same way.

## Structure

- Antithesis: "it's not X, it's Y", "not just X but Y". Say the positive thing.
- Copula avoidance: "serves as", "boasts" where "is" or "has" is the word.
- Bold as highlighter; bold labels.
- Tee-ups: "Here's the thing.", "The kicker?", a rhetorical opener. State the thing.
- Participle tails: "ensuring a smooth rollout". Keep the fact, cut at the comma.
- Objections nobody raised: "I'm not saying...".
- Template phrases and inflation: "in today's", "a game-changer".
- Crutch transitions and hedges: "moreover", "that said", "it's worth noting", "honestly".
- Chatbot residue: "great question", "happy to help", "I hope this email finds you well".
- Slogan fragments and rhythm by rule: stacked two-word sentences, forced triads, synonym cycling.

## Drafting

- Prompt echo: the draft repeats the request's wording ("wanted to be firm but kind here"). The user's own prose is exempt.
- Generic over specific: every supplied number, name and link appears.
- Over-smoothing: keep roughness the profile shows (lowercase starts, comma splices); never fake it.
- Uniform confidence: a flat line can sit beside a strong one.
- Over-compression: no arrows or decoder-ring abbreviations the profile doesn't use.

## Vocabulary

Press-release words die in a message: delve, leverage, robust, seamless, unlock, empower, showcase, elevate, streamline, foster, crucial, "deep dive", "learnings". Rewrite around the specific instead of swapping synonyms.

## Keep

Oddly specific details, unresolved mixed feelings, in-jokes, asides and self-corrections, and any flagged word inside a quotation or protected span.
